## Interpretation (descriptive only)

- **H1 supported.** Against an off-chain insider, PatchLedger detected or prevented every S1–S8 attempt. The editable store missed every content edit (S1, S2, S5).
- **H2 supported.** External checkpoints close the rewrite and deletion gap (S1–S3), but no centralized variant can stop the operator from writing records **in another party's name** (S7) or **forging an auditor's resolution** (S8). That gap comes from per-party keys and contract-enforced roles, not from hashing. Signed centralized logs could narrow it; they were not tested here.
- The hardened store's misses in S1, S3 and S5 are cases that touched the **newest** record: no later link exists to break.
- S4, S6, S9 and S10 are equal across systems. Evidence hashing, application-level role checks, digest-reuse lookup and conflict analysis are not blockchain properties.
- **H3 supported.** The chain costs about 200× more insert latency than SQLite even with instant automining, and about 1.4–2.2× more local bytes per record, plus on-chain state and about 135k gas.
- **Limitations:** one machine, one client, a local automining node, synthetic data, and a single attacker model. No statistical significance testing. See [experiment-protocol.md](../experiment-protocol.md#threats-to-validity).
